# Working at Height Procedure

## Scope
Applies to all work where a person could fall a distance liable to cause injury, including scaffolds, mobile
elevating work platforms (MEWPs), ladders, roofs, slab edges and floor openings.

## Edge protection
Open edges and floor openings must be protected by guardrails with a top rail, mid rail and toe board, or by
secured, marked covers able to take the expected load. Edge protection must not be removed without a permit;
where it is removed for access, an alternative control must be in place before work continues.

## Personal fall protection
Where collective protection is not practicable, workers must wear a full body harness attached to an approved
anchor point at all times while exposed to a fall. A rescue plan is required before any work in a harness.

## Access equipment
Scaffolds must be inspected by a competent person before first use, after alteration or bad weather, and at
least every 7 days, with the inspection recorded on the scaffold tag. Untagged scaffolds must not be used.
Ladders shall be used only for short-duration, low-risk work and must be secured against slipping.

## Permit to work
Work at height above 2 metres requires a work-at-height permit signed by the supervisor before work starts.

## Fragile roofs, rooflights and voids
Rooflights, skylights, fibre cement sheets and liner panels are fragile and will not carry a person. Fragile
areas must be covered, guarded or protected by safety netting or soft landing systems underneath, and nets or
covers must not be removed without a permit. Risers, lift shafts, stair voids and holes in slabs must have fixed
covers marked "hole below" or a guarded edge. Nobody may step back or walk across a roof without a planned,
protected route.

## Stepladders, hop-ups and podiums
Stepladders, hop-ups and podium steps must be on firm, level ground clear of debris and loose screed, used
within their rated height, and not over-reached from. Leaning ladders must be tied or footed and extend 1 metre
above the landing. A fall from steps or a low platform can still cause fractures and must be reported.

## Scaffold loading and dropped objects
Toe boards, brick guards and debris netting must be fitted where materials are stored or work is done at the
edge. Bars, blocks, boards, fixings and tools must not be stacked against an open edge. Tools used at height
must be tethered with tool lanyards. Areas below work at height, including bays under a deck or slab, must be
an exclusion zone with barriers and signs so that nobody walks under a falling object.

## Rescue and reporting
A person who has fallen, or been struck by a dropped or falling object, must receive first aid and the area
must be made safe. Falls through fragile surfaces, from edges, from ladders and from scaffolds, and objects
dropped from height, are reportable and must be investigated.
