# Hot Work, Confined Space and Manual Handling Procedure

## Hot work permit
Welding, cutting, grinding and any work producing sparks or flame is hot work and requires a hot work permit
signed by the supervisor before work starts. Combustible materials must be removed or covered within 10 metres.

## Fire watch
A fire watch must be posted during hot work and for at least 60 minutes after it stops. A suitable fire
extinguisher must be at the point of work.

## Confined space entry
Manholes, tanks, chambers and similar spaces are confined spaces. Entry requires a confined space permit, a gas
test before entry and continuous gas monitoring during the work. A standby person must stay at the entrance
and a rescue plan with rescue equipment must be in place before anyone enters.

## Manual handling
Tasks involving lifting or carrying heavy or awkward loads must have a manual handling assessment. Mechanical
aids such as goods lifts, trolleys and lifting equipment must be used where reasonably practicable. Loads above
25 kg per person shall not be lifted by hand without an assessment.

## Torch-on roofing and gas equipment
Torch-on membranes, gas torches, propane and LPG cylinders, disc cutters and grinders are hot work. Torching must
not be done near timber fascias, insulation, upstands or outlets without shielding; insulation such as PIR can
smoulder unseen under a membrane, so the fire watch must check for heat and smoke after torching stops. Gas
cylinders must be stored upright, chained and away from the work. If a fire starts, raise the alarm, evacuate to
the muster point, call the fire brigade and use an extinguisher only if safe.

## Atmospheres in pits, tanks and chambers
Lift pits, tanks, sumps, sewers, attenuation tanks, inspection chambers and basements with poor ventilation can
contain fumes, solvent vapour, carbon monoxide, hydrogen sulphide (H2S) or low oxygen. Solvent primers, tanking,
coatings and petrol or diesel engines must not be used inside without forced ventilation and gas monitoring. An
operative who feels dizzy, sick or is overcome must be rescued from outside without others entering unprotected.

## Lifting and carrying
Heavy bags, blocks, kerbs, plasterboard, glass, drums and materials must be moved with a goods hoist, trolley,
pallet truck, vacuum lifter or team lift. Carrying loads up stairs, twisting while lifting and repetitive lifting
cause back strain, hernias and musculoskeletal injuries. If the goods hoist is out of action, materials must be
held until it is repaired, not carried by hand.
